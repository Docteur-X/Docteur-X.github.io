# Educational principles

The student owns the mathematical strategy. The engine owns rule validity. The gesture interpreter proposes intent; the teaching policy may govern optional explanation or grouping, but must not override mathematical necessity. A proposed preview is not a commit. History preserves mathematical steps and alternative branches, not a guessed psychological account of student thought.

Visual conventions are instructional: green eligibility, amber proposed mathematical changes, blue grouping. They must be consistently rendered without implying that a hitbox rectangle is a mathematical bracket. Explicit AST grouping and precedence-required renderer parentheses have different roles.

An unusual valid route should remain available; rejected operations should have an intelligible reason when the engine can establish one. The long-term aim is a worksheet in which learners can annotate why they chose a step, compare alternatives and share reproducible reasoning.
