# Founding vision — student agency

> **The student chooses mathematical transformations; the software faithfully enforces mathematical rules and preserves the paths the student explores.**

The Manipulator aims to make symbolic mathematics on a computer feel as direct as manipulating symbols on paper. Its purpose is not to choose an optimal solution for the learner. The learner decides whether to rearrange, distribute, factorise, introduce an identity, apply an operation to an equality, or explore a different branch; the mathematical engine checks what is justified.

Three connected aims: **manipulate** mathematics through meaningful gestures; **express** mathematical reasoning through deliberate choices and branching work; **communicate** understanding through inspectable, reproducible histories, provenance and eventually annotations.

A valid transformation is not necessarily a useful strategy. Do not silently optimize or auto-simplify merely because the computer can. Feedback and teaching scaffolds may help, but must not replace agency. The software can record *what* happened and which rule justified it; it cannot infer the student's higher-level motivation from gestures alone.

**Non-negotiables:** mathematical correctness (including domains, assumptions, types, forward implication versus equivalence), stable node identities and provenance, non-mutating previews, accessible keyboard/touch paths, offline availability and practical performance on inexpensive older devices.

These principles are enduring design requirements, **not claims that all proposed features exist**. For the fuller original rationale see [archived founding statement](../06_Historical_Records/Archive/FOUNDING_VISION_STUDENT_AGENCY.md).
