## Purpose and enduring principles

The Manipulator is an offline-first, student-directed algebra workspace. Students choose their strategies; AST rules and the mathematical engine determine correctness; the interface explains structure, provenance and consequences. Preserve stable IDs for unchanged AST occurrences, branching per-object history, source-stamped single-use commits, accessibility, classroom performance and future extensibility to vectors, matrices and independent views.

Gesture recognition, mathematical validity and teaching policy are separate. Distribution targets the `+` operator by default; bracket targeting is only a possible future teaching-policy experiment. Visual feedback decorates; interactive affordances belong to the interaction layer if real examples justify them.

## Permanent documentation and AI handover policy (authoritative)

Documentation is part of the engineering work, not an optional final note. **Every meaningful implementation, audit, hardening patch, acceptance checkpoint, or consequential design decision must update the relevant living documentation without waiting for a user reminder.** The archive must remain sufficient for a competent developer or future AI assistant to resume safely without reconstructing decisions from chat history.

Maintain the following, proportionately to the change:

- **Vision and educational philosophy:** student agency, mathematical correctness and engine authority, explainable transformations, classroom accessibility and offline use, stable AST identities, and future vectors/matrices and independent views.
- **Verified current status:** exact accepted baseline and test evidence, distinguishing independently executed Node tests, user-confirmed browser tests, pending checks, and unverified assumptions. Never present a proposed or Node-only change as browser-verified.
- **Feature inventory:** implemented behaviour versus planned, experimental, deliberately deferred, or unsupported functionality; distinguish teaching-policy choices from mathematical validity.
- **Architecture and ownership:** actual modules, interfaces, dependencies, state/lifecycle owners, invariants, and the reasoning behind ordering constraints. Cross-check descriptions against source code; code and tests take precedence over stale documentation.
- **Decisions and alternatives:** record not only what was chosen, but why, what was rejected or deferred, and what new evidence would justify reconsideration. Avoid speculative universal managers or abstractions without concrete use cases.
- **Known issues and evidence gaps:** confirmed defects versus plausible risks, reproduction conditions, cleanup and asynchronous limitations, test weaknesses, active investigations, and workarounds where applicable.
- **Roadmap and handover:** current work, next steps, acceptance criteria, commands to verify, relevant files, and a concise orientation for a new developer or assistant.
- **Maintainable implementation notes:** high-quality explanatory comments for non-obvious code, updated developer guides and tests that describe meaningful behavioural guarantees.

**Single-source-of-truth discipline:** Keep `Current_Status.md` as the one authoritative status snapshot, this `AI_Handover.md` as the one authoritative technical handover, and `START_HERE_FOR_NEXT_CHAT.md` as the navigation entry point. Update these in place. Archive historical milestones clearly rather than appending competing headings labelled CURRENT. Preserve historical reasoning without treating archived checkpoints as live instructions.

**Discussion rule:** When a material ambiguity concerns educational philosophy, student gesture expectations, teaching policy, mathematical semantics, architectural ownership, or a consequential trade-off, ask the project owner and document the agreed decision. Do not silently invent a policy just because an implementation is technically possible.

**Release gate:** An increment is not fully handed over until its current source, tests, documentation, known limitations, and next action agree.
